# Pokémon and Adventure

## Pokémon changes

- **All 151 Pokémon are obtainable** in ordinary, unrandomized play, allowing a living Pokédex.
- Updated base stats using later games, including separate Special Attack and Special Defense.
- Updated the EXP awarded for defeating Pokémon using Generation VIII values.
- Increased catch rates for **Dragonair (27 → 45), Dragonite (9 → 45), and Raticate (90 → 127)**.
- Reworked level-up moves using **Let's Go, Pikachu!** as a basis, adapted to the moves available here.
- Pokémon can learn moves **on evolution** and **multiple moves at one level**. Gaining several levels at once offers moves from every gained level.
- Expanded level-one move lists for the Move Relearner.
- Updated **all TM compatibility lists to Generation VII**.
- Added Pokémon genders and **shiny Pokémon**, with normal shiny odds of **1 in 8,192**.
- Every species has its own normal and shiny colors.
- Starter Pikachu has maximum natural stats (DVs) and the boosted base stats of Partner Pikachu from Let's Go: **45 HP / 80 Attack / 50 Defense / 75 Special Attack / 60 Special Defense / 120 Speed**.
- Ordinary wild and opposing Pikachu keep their regular base stats. **Wild Pikachu can evolve**; it is no longer treated as the starter.

## Wild encounters

- Reworked species and levels throughout Kanto, drawing on Red, Blue, and Yellow for more variety.
- Expanded walking encounters to **12 possible entries per area**, up from 10.
- Wild levels vary around the area's usual level, by up to **7 levels either way**; low-level encounters have a smaller effective range.
- Adjusted early Route 1 and Route 22 encounters.
- Improved Pokédex habitat information, including Cerulean Cave.

## Trainers and rematches

- Rebuilt trainer teams throughout the adventure for a harder game, including in Classic mode.
- Pokémon on the same team can have different levels.
- Gym Leaders, the Elite Four, and the final rival have custom movesets.
- Added individual trainer names and updated class names, with Let's Go and anime references. Jessie and James have their own class.
- Trainer Pokémon have more varied natural stats, and selected opponents are shiny.
- A.J. has his Sandshrew again, and selected Gym Leaders use legendary Pokémon.
- The first Champion battle reaches approximately **level 85**.
- After becoming Champion, challenge the **Gym Leaders again in badge order**, with new dialogue and teams. Complete that round before returning to the League.
- The Elite Four and Champion have stronger rematch teams; the Champion's reach **level 130**.
- Giovanni's rematch team is **level 94–99**, including shiny Kangaskhan. Lance's Dragonite is shiny in both battles.

## Gifts and special encounters

**Event spoilers below.** Nuzlocke restrictions still apply to gifts and extra rewards.

- **Mew under the truck:** push the Vermilion dock truck left with Strength to reveal **level 50 Mew**, complete with a dust animation.
- The Magikarp sold near Mt. Moon is **shiny and level 15**. Scripted shiny gifts can have different genders where the species allows it.
- In Classic, take **both Mt. Moon fossils** and **both Fighting Dojo Pokémon**.
- Gift levels: **Bulbasaur, Charmander, and Squirtle 10; Eevee 30; Lapras 40; revived fossils 30**.
- Legendary bird levels: **Articuno 60, Zapdos 65, Moltres 70**.
- Receive **five Poké Balls along with the Pokédex**.
- Viridian's catching demonstration features Grandpa trying—and failing—to catch a **level 255 Mew**. He then takes you into the school, where the teacher gives you the **Type Chart**. The lesson and gift happen once.

## World changes

- Added a **PC at Day Care**, which also accepts Pokémon that know HM moves.
- Added front walls to the Celadon Mart and Mansion rooftops.
- Corrected the north–south Underground Path's size, Route 2 gate-exit tiles, a Viridian Cut tree, and the Route 16 sign interaction.
- Added post-Champion dialogue and the League guard for Gym rematch progression.
